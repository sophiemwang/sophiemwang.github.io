# CPSC 490 Submission
Sophie Wang
Advisor: Stephen Slade

Abstract:
This project investigates the intersection of professional food criticism and 
public sentiment by analyzing all Yelp reviews across fifty New York City restaurants 
reviewed by New York Times (NYT) food critic Pete Wells. Using a custom Python 
pipeline and restaurant domain-specific sentiment classifier, it tracks Yelp review 
sentiment patterns over time. The study finds that Wells’ “fair” rating coincides with 
statistically significant drops in review volume, suggesting reduced diner engagement rather 
than heightened diner negative criticism. In contrast, further analysis across categories such 
as price range revealed no statistically significant sentiment shifts after Wells’ reviews. 
The project also introduces a web-based tool that visualizes the most frequently mentioned 
words in reviews, filtered by sentiment and time frame, revealing trends and shifts in consumer 
priorities. Results also show that the domain-specific sentiment model more accurately aligns 
with star ratings, underscoring the importance of tailored NLP tools. This methodology has 
broader applications in understanding how expert and crowd-sourced opinions interact across industries.
